My Top 3 Traits

Trait 1:Kind

Being kind means treating others with care and respect. It includes helping people and being considerate of their feelings. Kindness matters to me because I want people to feel comfortable and valued around me. Even a small act of kindness can make someone’s day better. Being kind helps me build positive relationships with my classmates and teachers. It encourages me to help classmates who are struggling and treat others respectfully, even when we disagree.

Trait 2:Empathetic

Being empathetic means trying to understand how someone else feels and seeing a situation from their point of view. Empathy reminds me that people may be facing challenges I know nothing about. It helps me listen and understand them before judging their actions. Being empathetic helps me support classmates who feel stressed or overwhelmed. During group work, it helps me consider other people’s perspectives and be patient with them.

Trait 3:Stubborn

Being stubborn means holding firmly to my opinions or decisions and sometimes finding it difficult to change my mind. This trait helps me stay determined when something is difficult. However, I also recognise that I need to remain open to advice and admit when I am wrong. My stubbornness can motivate me to keep trying when an assignment is challenging. Sometimes, though, it can make accepting feedback or agreeing with group members difficult. I am learning to balance my determination with listening to others.

My Hero

Who they are

My hero is my mom. She is someone whose strength I have witnessed since I was young enough to understand what was happening around me.

Why they are my hero

My mom is my hero because I have watched her face many challenges throughout her life, yet she always finds a way to overcome them. She often goes through these difficulties quietly, without telling anyone, and still manages to smile. I admire her strength and the way she keeps going through hard times. Seeing her perseverance inspires me to keep trying when I face challenges of my own.

Favourite scene, motto or quote

“Pretty, I pray Allah blesses you in life.” My mom always says these words to me, and they mean a lot because they remind me of her love and her hopes for my future. Her prayer encourages me when I feel uncertain and reminds me that she wants the best for me.

LLMs: A Tool, Not a Crutch

What this statement means to me

“LLMs: A Tool, Not a Crutch” means that I should use artificial intelligence to support my learning while still thinking for myself. Large language models can help explain difficult topics, but I should make an effort to understand the explanations instead of simply copying answers. To me, learning means being able to explain something in my own words and apply it without depending on AI every time. This also means keeping responsibility for my work. UNESCO’s guidance encourages using AI in education in ways that protect learners’ ability to think and make decisions for themselves (Miao & Holmes, 2023).

When, where and how I can use LLMs to learn

I can use LLMs when studying at home, reviewing lessons after class, or preparing for tests. If I do not understand a topic, I can ask for a simpler explanation or an example, then compare it with my class notes. For example, when working on database exercises, I can ask an LLM to explain an SQL error and help me understand why it happened. I can then make the correction myself and check whether the query works. I can also ask for practice questions, attempt them on my own, and use the feedback to identify areas I need to improve. For written assignments, I can use an LLM to help organise my ideas or identify unclear sentences. However, the experiences, opinions and final wording should reflect my own understanding. I should also follow my lecturer’s rules about AI use and check information against reliable sources.

References

Miao, F., & Holmes, W. (2023). Guidance for generative AI in education and research. UNESCO. https://doi.org/10.54675/EWZM9535
